## PART 1 — How padel racket advice is done today

### 1.1 Proven facts: existing finder / comparison tools

**[F]** PadelReference "PalaMatch" is a recommender created by co-founder Pierre Loubic; it "analyses variables such as playing level, preferred playing style and individual preferences" and outputs personalised recommendations (padelmagazine.fr, PalaMatch article; padelreference.com/en/palamatch, acc.). PadelReference also runs a separate "Compare Padel Rackets" page.

**[F]** PadelVerdict scores every racket on eight parameters (Power, Spin, Control, Stability, Comfort, Maneuverability, Sweetspot, Playability), "weighted differently for attackers, defenders and hybrids, so the same racket can earn three different scores"; its comparator shows up to 3 rackets with radar charts, parameter scores and retailer links; it claims 183 rackets rated and "Brands don't pay, don't preview and don't get a vote" (padelverdict.com, /compare/, /rackets/, acc.).

**[F]** PadelRacketReviews.com scores six things (power, control, sweet spot, comfort, manoeuvrability, value) into a single "PRR score out of 100", refreshed weekly (padelracketreviews.com, acc.).

**[F]** PadelZoom (Spain) rates each racket on potencia, control, salida de bola, manejabilidad, punto dulce; users can submit their own analyses which "the algorithm processes to refine the global analysis"; its comparator compares up to 4 rackets on price, control, power and ball output (elreferente.es profile; padelzoom.es/comparador/, acc.). Their definition: salida de bola = "speed the ball leaves without effort"; manejabilidad = round shape + low balance = high.

**[F]** X-Compare (Extreme Tennis / Extreme Padel, France) claims to be "the first comparison tool entirely based on lab-tested data", up to 4 rackets on power, control, maneuverability, forgiveness, stability; "no personal opinions or subjective ratings" (padelmagazine.fr X-Compare article; extreme-tennis.eu/x-compare, acc.).

**[F]** Esprit Padel Shop "El Comparator": "compare the main characteristics of rackets and group the result in diagram form" (padelmagazine.fr, acc.).

**[F]** Racketguide.com "Racket Radar" markets itself as an AI-based comparator "with the best quality-price ratio" (padeladdict.com article; racketguide.com, acc.). PadelMatch.app: "intelligent recommender with conversational AI, finds your match in 2 minutes". PalaFinder.com and PalaHack: AI + price comparators. RacketFits: "in less than two minutes... level, style and budget".

**[F]** Padel Nuestro runs a brand-specific "Recomendador de palas Babolat" (padelnuestro.com/int/babolat-padel-racket-recommender, acc.); no generic Padel Nuestro finder was found.

**[F]** Tennis-Point racket advisor: "12 simple questions about playing style, fitness level, previous experience, and any injuries", outputs "six suitable models" tailored to age, gender and level (tennis-point.com/pages/racket-advisor, acc.). It also runs a Test Racket Service, and Padel-Point runs a "padel test track".

**[F]** Tennis Warehouse University: Racquet Comparison Tool (two racquets; static specs length/headsize/weight/balance/swingweight/flex/twistweight and dynamic specs power potential, sweet zone, vibration frequency); "Similar Playing Racquets" tool (finds racquets near a reference racquet in static and dynamic specs); Racquet Recommender (filter by performances). TWU also has a **Padel Racket Properties Tool** and "Compare Padel Rackets" page measuring swingweight, twistweight, power potential (ACOR), hittingweight and dynamic impact stiffness (twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/padel/padelspecs.php, acc.).

**[F]** HEAD has a "Padel Racquet Finder" (head.com/en/padel-racquet-finder); Wilson Racket Finder lets you "choose by racket attributes like weight, size and benefit, as well as by swing arc and swing speed" (au.wilson.com/pages/racket-finder, acc.). Retailer quizzes: Racket Central, NINEPADEL ("analyzes real racket specifications and compares them with best sellers"), Padel.fyi, Y1, Mantis, PadelCompass (adaptive: "beginners get a streamlined flow while advanced players get a more technical questionnaire").

**[F]** Bullpadel.com has a "registra tu pala" (register your racket) page; no Bullpadel-branded finder surfaced in search. Padel Addict "PadelFinder" and "Smash Expert" comparator did not surface; Smash-Expert.com is a French shop with guidance text only.

**[F]** PadeLMQ already offers a test-racket service: €100 deposit per racket, 5 days, return label, pickup/return at Padelclub Pachanga (Mechelen/Muizen) via WhatsApp (padelmq.com/en/pages/onze-test-mogelijkheden, acc.). Padel Point's programme: up to 3 rackets, 14 days, €8-10 per racket, incl. Belgium.

### 1.2 Proven facts: standard racket attributes

**[F]** Shape: round / teardrop / diamond determine sweet-spot position and balance (ltapadel.org.uk; padel.how sweet spot). **[F]** Balance thresholds commonly published: low ≤26 cm, medium ~26-27 cm, high ≥27 cm; beginners advised under 26.5 cm (toppadel.com; aol.com beginners article). **[F]** Weight by level: beginners <360 g, intermediate 360-370 g, advanced 370-390 g, women ~350-355 g "standard men's 365 g" (padelproshop.com weight blog; tiendapadel5.com; padelseries.com); body-weight rule of thumb <70 kg: 340-360 g, 70-85 kg: 360-375 g, >85 kg: 375 g+ (tiendapadelpoint.com). **[F]** Tolerance: "Babolat and Wilson state ±10 g, Kuikma ±5 g; Bullpadel, NOX and Siux quote a 10-15 g range instead of a tolerance"; "a racket sold as 365-375 g can arrive anywhere in that band, which is why some makers, Cork Padel and Royal Padel among them, weigh each racket and let you pick the exact gram" (padelsmith.co.uk weight guide, acc.). "Many padel stores do not offer the possibility of choosing the weight" (padelseries.com). Listed weight is "naked": a 360 g racket with one overgrip and protector becomes ~372-377 g; r/padel users report 390-400 g ready-to-play on rackets listed at 365 g (thepadelbrief.com, acc.). **[F]** Carbon "K" = thousands of filaments per tow; 3K firmer/drier, 12K/18K more elastic; "weave count affects feel far less than marketing suggests once you factor in the core, shape and balance" (padelbrowser.com; 12kpadel.com). **[F]** Rough vs smooth: "maybe 5-10 % more rotation... not transformative"; holes are not the main spin driver (padelpulseace.com; padelspeed.com). **[F]** Thickness 38 mm is the regulatory maximum and the de-facto standard (e.g., Vertex 05 2026 "38 mm profile", tradeinn/padelusa listings). **[F]** Tennis elbow: consensus is soft EVA core, low/medium balance, 355-370 g, round shape; "harder cores absorb less vibration"; medical disclaimer (padel.fyi; padelbears.co.uk; zonadepadel 2024/06). **[F]** Handedness: racket choice "is not based on laterality" (tiendapadelpoint FAQ); it matters only for side-of-court tactics. **[F]** Balance customisation is now a product feature: Nox AT10 2026 "Weight Balance System" with 2 g and 4 g inserts (padelshop.com AT10 2026 vs 2025 posts).

|---|---|---|---|

| Weight (listed) | 340-390 g; ranges like 365-375 g | Stability & power vs manoeuvrability & arm load | High, but *listed* ≠ *actual* |

### 1.3 Proven facts: model-year evolution and "upgrade from my current racket" reasoning

1. **Bullpadel Vertex 04 (2025) → Vertex 05 (2026)** [F]: both 365-375 g, balance ~25.5-26.5 cm, diamond, MultiEva core, 38 mm. 05 adds Curv:Aktiv side structure (torsional rigidity), Ease Vibe dampeners "absorb up to 49 % of vibrations", and reviewers measured "a 5-point swingweight drop... felt on reaction volleys and switches between defence and attack" (padelshop.com Vertex 04 vs 05; racketcentral.com; sportofpadel.com, acc.). Upgrade reasoning: "if you want a racket that feels faster, more responsive and more versatile, the 05 is a clear improvement"; if you are happy with 04 comfort, gain is marginal.
2. **Bullpadel Hack 03 (2024) → Hack 04 (2025/2026)** [F]: same diamond/high balance; 04 "vibrates less and feels more stable", improved Total Channel aerodynamics, refined MultiEva "smoother transition between power and control", "even more power on smash and volley" (padelshop.com Hack 03 vs 04). Hack 04 vs Vertex 05 2026: Hack "presses harder when you accelerate... more offensive and direct", Vertex 05 "combines power, control and a more balanced sensation" (zonadepadel 2026/07 and 2026/01). Hack 05 (2027) exists as a "first look" only (padelverdict.com).
3. **Nox AT10 12K vs 18K (2025 and 2026)** [F]: 18K = firmer, "dry, sharp, controlled", for good technique; 12K = "a touch more comfort and forgiveness" with same teardrop balance; 2026 adds aerodynamic mould, EOS Tunnel side holes, 30 mm extended handle, new spin texture, photochromic paint and Weight Balance System; Attack 12K moves from MLD Black EVA to HR3 Hard EVA (zonadepadel 2025/07, 2026/01, 2026/04; padelshop.com 2026-vs-2025 series; noxsport.com AT10 2026 series). Reviewers explicitly write switching logic: "once your swing has shortened and you're not flying balls long anymore, the AT10 Genius Attack 12K is a natural upgrade" (padelbrowser.com tennis-switchers guide).
4. **Siux Electra ST3 → ST4 (Stupa)** [F]: ST3 355-370 g, balance 26.5-27 cm, "EVA Hard, medium-hard vs medium-soft ST2"; ST4 355-375 g, balance ~26 cm, "double V" heart reinforcement replacing single horizontal bar, unperforated centre = larger sweet spot, more torsional rigidity; feel "softer than ST3, firmer than ST2"; caveat "mishits transmit more shock than a softer racket" (keepadel.com; findpadeluk.co.uk; padelracketreviews.com, acc.).

**[O]** Reviewers' upgrade logic always has the same skeleton: *same DNA (shape/weight/balance) → what changed (1-3 named things) → what you will feel (faster / plusher / larger sweet spot) → who should and should not bother.* That skeleton is the template for the advisor's "compare with what I have" answer.

Facts supporting answers: warranty commonly 6 months for Babolat/Bullpadel/Head/Adidas, range 3 months-2 years, manufacturing defects only, "cracks that start on the outside... are not covered", returns generally refused once handle plastic is removed, Prime Padel is an exception (7-day used return if trading up) (e-padel.com; tiendapadelpoint.com; padelusa.com; totalpadel.com; primepadelstore.com, acc.). ### 1.5 Hypotheses (Part 1)

**[H1]** The single most differentiating data asset for PadeLMQ is *per-unit* data: actual weight (and ideally balance) of each racket in stock, plus ready-to-play weight after the grip/protector the customer picks. No mainstream finder offers this; a few makers do. **[H2]** A "year-over-year delta" table (predecessor → successor, changed attributes, reviewer consensus verdict) is small (perhaps 60-100 rows for 7 brands × 2 seasons) and is what makes "should I upgrade?" answerable. **[H3]** Most quiz abandonment comes from asking level/style questions that the customer's current racket already answers; the current racket (or the last 2 they liked/disliked) is a stronger input than any self-assessment. **[H4]** Scores (radar numbers) are interpretations; if the advisor shows them it must label the source (lab, editorial, shop staff) or trust will erode as customers see different sites disagree.

## PART 2 — Interface patterns that are not a text chat

### 2.1 Proven facts from other verticals

**[F]** TWU "Similar Playing Racquets": pick a reference racquet, get racquets nearest in weight/balance/swingweight and in power potential/sweet-spot size; Performance Analysis shows "where any racquet lives within the racquet universe" (scatter). **[F]** Bike Insights and 99 Spokes: "interactive geometry drawing overlay... compare frame dimensions of different models and sizes"; 99 Spokes adds a price-vs-spec scatter plot and gearing charts; data "sourced directly from manufacturers and from millions of people" (bicycleretailer.com 2019-08-05; bikeinsights.com/compare, acc.). Geometry Geeks: 9,000+ bikes, search by stack & reach, user-added bikes, paid advanced tier (geometrygeeks.bike). **[F]** PING WebFit: answer questions + biometrics (height, wrist-to-floor, hand size), ~90 s per club type, emailed report, then "share your results with a certified PING fitter to fine-tune" (ping.com/en-us/fitting/webfit; mygolfspy.com, acc.). TaylorMade SelectFit is an in-person adjustable-head kit (loft/lie ±3°) rather than an online tool. **[F]** Fleet Feet fit id (Volumental): 3-second 3D scan, 12 measurements, compared with hundreds of thousands of scans, result emailed; 5 million scans (volumental.com; fleetfeet.com/fit-process). **[F]** Nike Fit (2019): 13-point AR scan, then per-shoe size advice with social proof phrasing: "80 percent of people with similar feet to you purchased this size" / "this shoe runs slightly small" (dezeen.com 2019-05-09; cnbc.com). **[F]** Warby Parker: quiz on face shape and style → recommended frames → AR try-on and home try-on, "mix and match all three methods" (warbyparker.com). **[F]** Configurators (Nike By You, IKEA planner, Tesla): live preview, running subtotal, mobile-first, "reduce uncertainty by letting customers see exactly what they are designing" (londondynamics.com; ironplane.com; commerce-ui.com). 

**[F-full]** Generative UI tooling: Vercel AI SDK UI is "a set of hooks that help you build chatbots and generative user interfaces", rendering components from tool-invocation states (github.com/vercel/ai). vercel-labs/json-render: "AI generates interfaces from natural language prompts, constrained to components you define"; catalog of components + actions, `setState`, watchers, progressive "SpecStream" rendering; "Guardrailed - AI can only use components in your catalog" (github.com/vercel-labs/json-render). CopilotKit: "agents generate and update UI components dynamically at runtime based on user intent and agent state"; backend tool rendering; human-in-the-loop pauses for confirmation (github.com/CopilotKit/CopilotKit). Commerce assistants (Amazon Rufus, Klarna) advertise "compare products" but published material shows text/list output, not rich comparison widgets (aboutamazon.com; fin.ai roundup).

### 2.2 Observations: what makes these feel like a good salesperson

**[O1]** They start from an anchor the customer already owns (reference racquet, your bike, your foot scan) and express everything as *deltas from that anchor*, not absolutes. **[O2]** They show the trade-off shape, not a winner: overlay drawings, scatter plots, two-column spec tables with highlighted differences. **[O3]** They give a reason in the customer's words plus social proof ("runs small", "80 % of people like you"). **[O4]** They hand off to a human/physical step (PING fitter, Fleet Feet store, Warby home try-on) as the natural next step, which PadeLMQ already has (test service, Pachanga). **[O5]** Configurators keep a persistent "current build" panel with a running total, and let you fork/save/share.

### 2.3 Design recommendations: a "virtual padel workbench"

**[R1] Anchor-first, quiz-last.** First screen: "What do you play with now?" (search-as-you-type over all brands, incl. discontinued; fallback "no racket / tennis player / kids"). Derive level, style and preferences from the anchor's known attributes; ask at most 3 follow-ups (what do you want more of: power / control / comfort; any elbow pain; budget). Reuse Tennis-Point's injury question but not its 12 steps.

**[R2] Delta cards instead of score radars.** For each candidate, a card showing the same six rows (shape, weight band + *actual unit weight in stock*, balance, core, face, surface) with the candidate value, the anchor value, and a one-word delta chip ("+5 g", "softer", "higher balance") plus a plain-language "what you'll feel" line. Radars only as an optional overlay, labelled with their source (PadelVerdict/PadelZoom/lab).

**[R3] Model-year timeline.** For any racket in the catalogue, a horizontal timeline (Vertex 03 → 04 → 05) where each hop lists the 1-3 named changes and the review-consensus verdict ("faster, plusher; same DNA"). This answers pattern 16-19 directly and is a small curated dataset (H2).

**[R4] Weight bench.** A slider showing listed range, the actual weights of units on the shelf, and the ready-to-play weight after the grip/overgrip/protector the customer selects (Nike By You-style live preview, IKEA-style running total). This converts question 1-4 into a click ("reserve the 366 g unit"). It requires weighing incoming stock; adding a Shopify metafield per unit/variant.

**[R5] Workbench state panel.** Persistent side panel like a configurator: anchor racket, shortlisted candidates (max 3, like PadelVerdict), chosen grip build, and two buttons: "Book a test set" (pre-filled with the shortlist, €100 deposit rules shown) and "Ask the shop" (WhatsApp deep link with the workbench state serialised). Save/share via URL.

**[R6] Generative UI, constrained.** Implement with a fixed component catalogue (json-render style): DeltaCard, WeightBench, Timeline, PolicyAnswer (warranty/returns/shipping with citation to the policy page), ProductCarousel, TestBooking. The LLM chooses and fills components; it never free-writes specs. Human-in-the-loop confirmation before anything transactional (CopilotKit pattern). Text answers only for the long-tail questions (grip, balls, shoes), each backed by a Fact Brain entry with a source.

**[R7] Fact Brain schema (minimum).** Racket master (brand, model, year, shape, weight band, balance mm, core name + normalised soft/med/hard, face material + K, surface, frame tech names, thickness, level tag, elbow-friendly flag, predecessor_id, successor_id, change_notes[], review_scores{source, params}, unit_weights[]), Policy facts (warranty per brand, return rules, test-service rules, shipping to BE/NL/FR, VAT note), Accessory rules (grip layers by hand size, ball types by court/temperature, sole by surface), and an explicit `confidence`/`source` per field so the UI can label fact vs interpretation (Table B).

**[R8] Measure what tools don't.** Track quiz drop-off vs anchor-first completion, share of sessions that reach a test booking, and how often the "actual unit weight" component is opened; H1-H3 are cheap to falsify in 4-6 weeks.
